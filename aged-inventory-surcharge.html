The Amazon Aged Inventory Surcharge: What It Actually Costs You

If you sell wholesale on Amazon, you've probably heard the number "365 days" thrown around for aged inventory. Here's the part most sellers miss: the surcharge doesn't wait for the one-year mark. It ramps up in stages well before that, and by the time a unit hits the long-term storage threshold, it's often already cost you more in monthly fees than it's worth.

Why this sneaks past most sellers

Amazon's aged inventory fee stacks on top of your regular monthly storage fee — it doesn't replace it. So a unit that looked fine in your spreadsheet six months ago can quietly flip from profitable to a net loss, and nothing on your dashboard flags it. You have to go looking.

The real cost, in plain terms

For most standard-size items, aged inventory adds somewhere in the $10–$18 per unit per year range on top of normal storage — more for bulky or oversized items. If you've got 60 units sitting past six months, that's $600–$1,080 a year, on stock that may not even be moving.

What to actually do about it

  1. Pull your Inventory Age report in Seller Central.
  2. Flag anything past 180 days.
  3. For each one, ask: is this still profitable once the aged surcharge is added in? If not, it's a candidate to reprice, liquidate, or remove — before the fee climbs further.
